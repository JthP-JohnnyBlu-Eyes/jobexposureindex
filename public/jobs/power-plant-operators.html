Questions about power plant operators and AI

Will AI replace power plant operators?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Power Plant Operators score 23 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #609 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 11 of the job's 30 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 19 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which power plant operators tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Record and compile operational data by completing and maintaining forms, logs, or reports"; "Receive outage calls and request necessary personnel during power outages or emergencies"; "Prepare and submit compliance, operational, and safety forms or reports".

Which power plant operators tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Adjust controls to generate specified electrical power or to regulate the flow of power between generating stations and substations"; "Control generator output to match the phase, frequency, or voltage of electricity supplied to panels"; "Control or maintain auxiliary equipment, such as pumps, fans, compressors, condensers, feedwater heaters, filters, or chlorinators, to supply water, fuel, lubricants, air, or auxiliary power".

Are people already using AI for this work?

How many people work as power plant operators, and what do they earn?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
