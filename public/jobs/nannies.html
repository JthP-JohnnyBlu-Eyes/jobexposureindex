Questions about nannies and AI

Will AI replace nannies?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Nannies score 14 out of 100 (low exposure), #709 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 3 of the job's 19 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 16 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which nannies tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Supervise and assist with homework"; "Keep records of play, meal schedules, and bill payment".

Which nannies tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Remove hazards and develop appropriate boundaries and rules to create a safe environment for children"; "Instruct and assist children in the development of health and personal habits, such as eating, resting, and toilet behavior"; "Instruct children in safe behavior, such as seeking adult assistance when crossing the street and avoiding contact with unsafe objects".

Are people already using AI for this work?

How many people work as nannies, and what do they earn?

About 518,910 people in the US (figure for Childcare Workers), with median pay of $34,980 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
