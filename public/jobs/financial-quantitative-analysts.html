Questions about financial quantitative analysts and AI

Will AI replace financial quantitative analysts?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Financial Quantitative Analysts score 69 out of 100 (high exposure), #50 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 21 of the job's 21 tasks, 7 of them on its own. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which financial quantitative analysts tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Develop core analytical capabilities or model libraries, using advanced statistical, quantitative, or econometric techniques"; "Maintain or modify all financial analytic models in use"; "Apply mathematical or statistical techniques to address practical issues in finance, such as derivative valuation, securities trading, risk management, or financial market regulation".

Which financial quantitative analysts tasks are hardest for AI?

Every task in this job has some AI exposure, though 14 of them need extra software built around the AI.

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 7 of the 21 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as financial quantitative analysts, and what do they earn?

About 132,130 people in the US (figure for Financial Specialists, All Other), with median pay of $81,100 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
