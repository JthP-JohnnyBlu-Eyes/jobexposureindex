Questions about audio and video technicians and AI

Will AI replace audio and video technicians?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. Audio and Video Technicians score 44 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #359 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 23 of the job's 29 tasks, 5 of them on its own, while 6 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which audio and video technicians tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Maintain inventories of audio and videotapes and related supplies"; "Perform narration of productions or present announcements"; "Inform users of audio and videotaping service policies and procedures".

Which audio and video technicians tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Notify supervisors when major equipment repairs are needed"; "Direct and coordinate activities of assistants and other personnel during production"; "Perform minor repairs and routine cleaning of audio and video equipment".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 8 of the 29 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as audio and video technicians, and what do they earn?

About 70,230 people in the US, with median pay of $58,100 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
