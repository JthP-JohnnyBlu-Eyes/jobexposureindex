Questions about industrial-organizational psychologists and AI

Will AI replace industrial-organizational psychologists?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. Industrial-Organizational Psychologists score 49 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #255 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 22 of the job's 25 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 3 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which industrial-organizational psychologists tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Write articles, white papers, or reports to share research findings and educate others"; "Write reports on research findings and implications to contribute to general knowledge or to suggest potential changes in organizational functioning".

Which industrial-organizational psychologists tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Observe and interview workers to obtain information about the physical, mental, and educational requirements of jobs, as well as information about aspects such as job satisfaction"; "Coach senior executives and managers on leadership and performance"; "Counsel workers about job and career-related issues".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 10 of the 25 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as industrial-organizational psychologists, and what do they earn?

About 790 people in the US, with median pay of $193,950 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
