Questions about passenger attendants and AI

Will AI replace passenger attendants?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Passenger Attendants score 22 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #619 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 4 of the job's 14 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 10 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which passenger attendants tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Respond to passengers' questions, requests, or complaints"; "Greet passengers boarding transportation equipment and announce routes and stops".

Which passenger attendants tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Secure passengers for transportation by buckling seatbelts or fastening wheelchairs with tie-down straps"; "Provide boarding assistance to elderly, sick, or injured people"; "Determine or facilitate seating arrangements".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 3 of the 14 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as passenger attendants, and what do they earn?

About 27,110 people in the US, with median pay of $37,720 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
