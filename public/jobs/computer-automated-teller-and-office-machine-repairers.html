Questions about computer, automated teller, and office machine repairers and AI

Will AI replace computer, automated teller, and office machine repairers?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Computer, Automated Teller, and Office Machine Repairers score 37 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #455 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 11 of the job's 25 tasks, 7 of them on its own, while 14 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which computer, automated teller, and office machine repairers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Advise customers concerning equipment operation, maintenance, or programming"; "Reinstall software programs or adjust settings on existing software to fix machine malfunctions"; "Maintain records of equipment maintenance work or repairs".

Which computer, automated teller, and office machine repairers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Reassemble machines after making repairs or replacing parts"; "Disassemble machines to examine parts, such as wires, gears, or bearings for wear or defects, using hand or power tools and measuring devices"; "Align, adjust, or calibrate equipment according to specifications".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 5 of the 25 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as computer, automated teller, and office machine repairers, and what do they earn?

About 65,600 people in the US, with median pay of $47,810 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
