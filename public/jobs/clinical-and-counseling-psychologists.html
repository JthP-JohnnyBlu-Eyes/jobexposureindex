Questions about clinical and counseling psychologists and AI

Will AI replace clinical and counseling psychologists?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Clinical and Counseling Psychologists score 37 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #451 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 20 of the job's 30 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 10 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which clinical and counseling psychologists tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Write reports on clients and maintain required paperwork"; "Document patient information including session notes, progress notes, recommendations, and treatment plans"; "Prepare written evaluations of individuals' psychological competence for court hearings".

Which clinical and counseling psychologists tasks are hardest for AI?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
