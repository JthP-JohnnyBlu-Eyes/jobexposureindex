Questions about airfield operations specialists and AI

Will AI replace airfield operations specialists?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Airfield Operations Specialists score 37 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #459 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 19 of the job's 27 tasks, 4 of them on its own, while 8 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which airfield operations specialists tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Receive, transmit, and control message traffic"; "Maintain air-to-ground and point-to-point radio contact with aircraft commanders"; "Conduct departure and arrival briefings".

Which airfield operations specialists tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Implement airfield safety procedures to ensure a safe operating environment for personnel and aircraft operation"; "Assist in responding to aircraft and medical emergencies"; "Initiate or conduct airport-wide coordination of snow removal on runways and taxiways".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Not much yet. None of the job's tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude in April–May 2026, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as airfield operations specialists, and what do they earn?

About 15,190 people in the US, with median pay of $56,850 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023).
