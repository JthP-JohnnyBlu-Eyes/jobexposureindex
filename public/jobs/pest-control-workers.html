Questions about pest control workers and AI

Will AI replace pest control workers?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Pest Control Workers score 25 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #581 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 4 of the job's 15 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 11 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which pest control workers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Record work activities performed"; "Measure area dimensions requiring treatment, calculate fumigant requirements, and estimate cost for service".

Which pest control workers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Inspect premises to identify infestation source and extent of damage to property, wall, or roof porosity and access to infested locations"; "Spray or dust chemical solutions, powders, or gases into rooms, onto clothing, furnishings, or wood, or over marshlands, ditches, or catch basins"; "Clean work site after completion of job".

Are people already using AI for this work?

How many people work as pest control workers, and what do they earn?

About 102,620 people in the US, with median pay of $45,250 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
