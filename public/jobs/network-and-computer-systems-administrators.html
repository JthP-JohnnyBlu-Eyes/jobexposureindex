Questions about network and computer systems administrators and AI

Will AI replace network and computer systems administrators?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Network and Computer Systems Administrators score 73 out of 100 (high exposure), #38 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 18 of the job's 20 tasks, 10 of them on its own, while 2 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which network and computer systems administrators tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Maintain and administer computer networks and related computing environments, including computer hardware, systems software, applications software, and all configurations"; "Perform data backups and disaster recovery operations"; "Diagnose, troubleshoot, and resolve hardware, software, or other network and system problems, and replace defective components when necessary".

Which network and computer systems administrators tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Load computer tapes and disks, and install software and printer paper or forms"; "Maintain an inventory of parts for emergency repairs".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 14 of the 20 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as network and computer systems administrators, and what do they earn?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
