Questions about cardiovascular technologists and technicians and AI

Will AI replace cardiovascular technologists and technicians?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Cardiovascular Technologists and Technicians score 36 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #465 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 17 of the job's 25 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 8 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which cardiovascular technologists and technicians tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Transcribe, type, and distribute reports of diagnostic procedures for interpretation by physician"; "Enter factors, such as amount and quality of radiation beam, and filming sequence, into computer"; "Perform general administrative tasks, such as scheduling appointments or ordering supplies or equipment".

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
