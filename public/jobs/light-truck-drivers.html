Questions about light truck drivers and AI

Will AI replace light truck drivers?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Light Truck Drivers score 37 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #460 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 7 of the job's 14 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 7 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which light truck drivers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Maintain records, such as vehicle logs, records of cargo, or billing statements, in accordance with regulations"; "Report any mechanical problems encountered with vehicles"; "Report delays, accidents, or other traffic and transportation situations to bases or other vehicles, using telephones or mobile two-way radios".

Which light truck drivers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Obey traffic laws and follow established traffic and transportation procedures"; "Turn in receipts and money received from deliveries"; "Load and unload trucks, vans, or automobiles".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 1 of the 14 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as light truck drivers, and what do they earn?

About 983,300 people in the US, with median pay of $44,860 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
