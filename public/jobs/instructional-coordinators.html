Questions about instructional coordinators and AI

Will AI replace instructional coordinators?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. Instructional Coordinators score 49 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #264 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 26 of the job's 30 tasks, 6 of them on its own, while 4 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which instructional coordinators tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Interpret and enforce provisions of state education codes and rules and regulations of state education boards"; "Prepare or approve manuals, guidelines, and reports on state educational policies and practices for distribution to school districts"; "Define instructional, learning, or performance objectives".

Which instructional coordinators tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Observe work of teaching staff to evaluate performance and to recommend changes that could strengthen teaching skills"; "Plan and conduct teacher training programs and conferences dealing with new classroom procedures, instructional materials and equipment, and teaching aids"; "Conduct or participate in workshops, committees, and conferences designed to promote the intellectual, social, and physical welfare of students".

Are people already using AI for this work?

How many people work as instructional coordinators, and what do they earn?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023).
