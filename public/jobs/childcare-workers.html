Questions about childcare workers and AI

Will AI replace childcare workers?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Childcare Workers score 15 out of 100 (low exposure), #690 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 5 of the job's 25 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 20 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which childcare workers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Keep records on individual children, including daily observations and information about activities, meals served, and medications administered"; "Perform general administrative tasks, such as taking attendance, editing internal paperwork, and making phone calls"; "Help children with homework and school work".

Which childcare workers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Maintain a safe play environment"; "Observe and monitor children's play activities"; "Communicate with children's parents or guardians about daily activities, behaviors, and related issues".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 6 of the 25 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as childcare workers, and what do they earn?

About 518,910 people in the US, with median pay of $34,980 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
