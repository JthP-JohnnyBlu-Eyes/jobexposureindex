Questions about nuclear technicians and AI

Will AI replace nuclear technicians?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Nuclear Technicians score 14 out of 100 (low exposure), #694 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 5 of the job's 21 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 16 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which nuclear technicians tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Calculate equipment operating factors, such as radiation times, dosages, temperatures, gamma intensities, or pressures, using standard formulas and conversion tables"; "Prepare reports to communicate information such as contamination test results, decontamination results, or decontamination procedures".

Which nuclear technicians tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Follow nuclear equipment operational policies and procedures that ensure environmental safety"; "Conduct surveillance testing to determine safety of nuclear equipment"; "Test plant equipment to ensure it is operating properly".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Not much yet. None of the job's tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude in April–May 2026, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as nuclear technicians, and what do they earn?

About 6,470 people in the US, with median pay of $110,240 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
