Questions about robotics engineers and AI

Will AI replace robotics engineers?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. Robotics Engineers score 59 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #100 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 19 of the job's 24 tasks, 9 of them on its own, while 5 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which robotics engineers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Review or approve designs, calculations, or cost estimates"; "Debug robotics programs"; "Create back-ups of robot programs or parameters".

Which robotics engineers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Build, configure, or test robots or robotic applications"; "Supervise technologists, technicians, or other engineers"; "Investigate mechanical failures or unexpected maintenance problems".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 6 of the 24 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as robotics engineers, and what do they earn?

About 154,070 people in the US (figure for Engineers, All Other), with median pay of $122,930 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
