Questions about dental assistants and AI

Will AI replace dental assistants?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Dental Assistants score 27 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #565 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 6 of the job's 16 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 10 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which dental assistants tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Record treatment information in patient records"; "Provide postoperative instructions prescribed by dentist".

Which dental assistants tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Prepare patient, sterilize or disinfect instruments, set up instrument trays, prepare materials, or assist dentist during dental procedures"; "Assist dentist in management of medical or dental emergencies"; "Instruct patients in oral hygiene and plaque control programs".

Are people already using AI for this work?

How many people work as dental assistants, and what do they earn?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023).
