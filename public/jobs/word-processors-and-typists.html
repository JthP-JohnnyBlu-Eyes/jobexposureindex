Questions about word processors and typists and AI

Will AI replace word processors and typists?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Word Processors and Typists score 74 out of 100 (high exposure), #37 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 16 of the job's 20 tasks, 14 of them on its own, while 4 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which word processors and typists tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Check completed work for spelling, grammar, punctuation, and format"; "File and store completed documents on computer hard drive or disk, or maintain a computer filing system to store, retrieve, update, and delete documents"; "Address envelopes or prepare envelope labels, using typewriter or computer".

Which word processors and typists tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Perform other clerical duties, such as answering telephone, sorting and distributing mail, running errands or sending faxes"; "Print and make copies of work"; "Transmit work electronically to other locations".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 14 of the 20 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as word processors and typists, and what do they earn?

About 35,010 people in the US, with median pay of $49,280 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
