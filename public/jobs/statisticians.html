Questions about statisticians and AI

Will AI replace statisticians?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Statisticians score 79 out of 100 (high exposure), #30 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 19 of the job's 19 tasks, 11 of them on its own. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which statisticians tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Determine whether statistical methods are appropriate, based on user needs or research questions of interest"; "Prepare data for processing by organizing information, checking for inaccuracies, and adjusting and weighting the raw data"; "Present statistical and nonstatistical results, using charts, bullets, and graphs, in meetings or conferences to audiences such as clients, peers, and students".

Which statisticians tasks are hardest for AI?

Every task in this job has some AI exposure, though 8 of them need extra software built around the AI.

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 14 of the 19 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as statisticians, and what do they earn?

About 29,030 people in the US, with median pay of $105,650 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
