Questions about shuttle drivers and chauffeurs and AI

Will AI replace shuttle drivers and chauffeurs?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Shuttle Drivers and Chauffeurs score 25 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #581 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 8 of the job's 26 tasks, 5 of them on its own, while 18 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which shuttle drivers and chauffeurs tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Communicate with dispatchers by radio, telephone, or computer to exchange information and receive requests for passenger service"; "Complete accident reports when necessary"; "Maintain knowledge of first-aid procedures".

Which shuttle drivers and chauffeurs tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Check the condition of a vehicle's tires, brakes, windshield wipers, lights, oil, fuel, water, and safety equipment to ensure that everything is in working order"; "Collect fares or vouchers from passengers, and make change or issue receipts as necessary"; "Comply with traffic regulations to operate vehicles in a safe and courteous manner".

Are people already using AI for this work?

How many people work as shuttle drivers and chauffeurs, and what do they earn?

About 248,530 people in the US, with median pay of $37,290 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
