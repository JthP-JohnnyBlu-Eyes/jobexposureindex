Questions about occupational therapy assistants and AI

Will AI replace occupational therapy assistants?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Occupational Therapy Assistants score 27 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #564 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 8 of the job's 22 tasks, 4 of them on its own, while 14 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which occupational therapy assistants tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Report to supervisors, verbally or in writing, on patients' progress, attitudes, and behavior"; "Observe and record patients' progress, attitudes, and behavior and maintain this information in client records"; "Attend continuing education classes".

Which occupational therapy assistants tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Instruct, or assist in instructing, patients and families in home programs, basic living skills, or the care and use of adaptive equipment"; "Maintain and promote a positive attitude toward clients and their treatment programs"; "Implement, or assist occupational therapists with implementing, treatment plans designed to help clients function independently".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 4 of the 22 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as occupational therapy assistants, and what do they earn?

About 51,290 people in the US, with median pay of $72,300 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
