Questions about food science technicians and AI

Will AI replace food science technicians?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Food Science Technicians score 36 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #466 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 7 of the job's 16 tasks, 4 of them on its own, while 9 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which food science technicians tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Record or compile test results or prepare graphs, charts, or reports"; "Maintain records of testing results or other documents as required by state or other governing agencies"; "Compute moisture or salt content, percentages of ingredients, formulas, or other product factors, using mathematical and chemical procedures".

Which food science technicians tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Conduct standardized tests on food, beverages, additives, or preservatives to ensure compliance with standards and regulations regarding factors such as color, texture, or nutrients"; "Taste or smell foods or beverages to ensure that flavors meet specifications or to select samples with specific characteristics"; "Monitor and control temperature of products".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 3 of the 16 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as food science technicians, and what do they earn?

About 14,600 people in the US, with median pay of $52,130 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023).
