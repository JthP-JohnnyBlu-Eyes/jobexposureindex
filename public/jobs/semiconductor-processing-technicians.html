Questions about semiconductor processing technicians and AI

Will AI replace semiconductor processing technicians?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Semiconductor Processing Technicians score 23 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #608 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 6 of the job's 24 tasks, 4 of them on its own, while 18 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which semiconductor processing technicians tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Maintain processing, production, and inspection information and reports"; "Study work orders, instructions, formulas, and processing charts to determine specifications and sequence of operations"; "Monitor operation and adjust controls of processing machines and equipment to produce compositions with specific electronic properties, using computer terminals".

Which semiconductor processing technicians tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Manipulate valves, switches, and buttons, or key commands into control panels to start semiconductor processing cycles"; "Clean semiconductor wafers using cleaning equipment, such as chemical baths, automatic wafer cleaners, or blow-off wands"; "Load and unload equipment chambers and transport finished product to storage or to area for further processing".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Not much yet. None of the job's tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude in April–May 2026, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as semiconductor processing technicians, and what do they earn?

About 31,460 people in the US, with median pay of $51,430 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
