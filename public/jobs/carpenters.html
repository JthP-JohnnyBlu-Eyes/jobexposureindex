Questions about carpenters and AI

Will AI replace carpenters?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Carpenters score 14 out of 100 (low exposure), #693 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 7 of the job's 29 tasks, 1 of them on its own, while 22 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which carpenters tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Maintain records, document actions, and present written progress reports".

Which carpenters tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Follow established safety rules and regulations and maintain a safe and clean environment"; "Measure and mark cutting lines on materials, using a ruler, pencil, chalk, and marking gauge"; "Assemble and fasten materials to make frameworks or props, using hand tools and wood screws, nails, dowel pins, or glue".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 2 of the 29 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as carpenters, and what do they earn?

About 670,090 people in the US, with median pay of $60,580 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
