Questions about occupational therapy aides and AI

Will AI replace occupational therapy aides?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Occupational Therapy Aides score 24 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #595 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 4 of the job's 15 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 11 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which occupational therapy aides tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Report to supervisors or therapists, verbally or in writing, on patients' progress, attitudes, attendance, and accomplishments"; "Observe patients' attendance, progress, attitudes, and accomplishments and record and maintain information in client records"; "Perform clerical, administrative, and secretarial duties, such as answering phones, restocking and ordering supplies, filling out paperwork, and scheduling appointments".

Which occupational therapy aides tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Encourage patients and attend to their physical needs to facilitate the attainment of therapeutic goals"; "Prepare and maintain work area, materials, and equipment and maintain inventory of treatment and educational supplies"; "Transport patients to and from the occupational therapy work area".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Not much yet. None of the job's tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude in April–May 2026, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as occupational therapy aides, and what do they earn?

About 4,310 people in the US, with median pay of $39,160 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
