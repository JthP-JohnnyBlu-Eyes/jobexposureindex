Questions about computer systems analysts and AI

Will AI replace computer systems analysts?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Computer Systems Analysts score 75 out of 100 (high exposure), #34 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 20 of the job's 22 tasks, 13 of them on its own, while 2 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which computer systems analysts tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Test, maintain, and monitor computer programs and systems, including coordinating the installation of computer programs and systems"; "Troubleshoot program and system malfunctions to restore normal functioning"; "Expand or modify system to serve new purposes or improve work flow".

Which computer systems analysts tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Consult with management to ensure agreement on system principles"; "Interview or survey workers, observe job performance, or perform the job to determine what information is processed and how it is processed".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 16 of the 22 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as computer systems analysts, and what do they earn?

About 519,530 people in the US, with median pay of $105,850 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
