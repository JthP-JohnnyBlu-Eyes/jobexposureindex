Questions about transportation security screeners and AI

Will AI replace transportation security screeners?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Transportation Security Screeners score 26 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #576 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 14 of the job's 26 tasks, 0 of them on its own, while 12 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which transportation security screeners tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Inspect carry-on items, using x-ray viewing equipment, to determine whether items contain objects that warrant further investigation"; "Check passengers' tickets to ensure that they are valid, and to determine whether passengers have designations that require special handling, such as providing photo identification"; "Test baggage for any explosive materials, using equipment such as explosive detection machines or chemical swab systems".

Which transportation security screeners tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Search carry-on or checked baggage by hand when it is suspected to contain prohibited items such as weapons"; "Perform pat-down or hand-held wand searches of passengers who have triggered machine alarms, who are unable to pass through metal detectors, or who have been randomly identified for such searches"; "Notify supervisors or other appropriate personnel when security breaches occur".

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023).
