Questions about elevator and escalator installers and repairers and AI

Will AI replace elevator and escalator installers and repairers?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Elevator and Escalator Installers and Repairers score 18 out of 100 (low exposure), #649 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 4 of the job's 20 tasks, 3 of them on its own, while 16 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which elevator and escalator installers and repairers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Check that safety regulations and building codes are met, and complete service reports verifying conformance to standards"; "Maintain log books that detail all repairs and checks performed"; "Participate in additional training to keep skills up to date".

Which elevator and escalator installers and repairers tasks are hardest for AI?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
