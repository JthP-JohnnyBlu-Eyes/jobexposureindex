Questions about computer numerically controlled tool operators and AI

Will AI replace computer numerically controlled tool operators?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Computer Numerically Controlled Tool Operators score 25 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #581 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 8 of the job's 27 tasks, 6 of them on its own, while 19 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which computer numerically controlled tool operators tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Transfer commands from servers to computer numerical control (CNC) modules, using computer network links"; "Enter commands or load control media, such as tapes, cards, or disks, into machine controllers to retrieve programmed instructions"; "Modify cutting programs to account for problems encountered during operation, and save modified programs".

Which computer numerically controlled tool operators tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Measure dimensions of finished workpieces to ensure conformance to specifications, using precision measuring instruments, templates, and fixtures"; "Mount, install, align, and secure tools, attachments, fixtures, and workpieces on machines, using hand tools and precision measuring instruments"; "Stop machines to remove finished workpieces or to change tooling, setup, or workpiece placement, according to required machining sequences".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 3 of the 27 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as computer numerically controlled tool operators, and what do they earn?

About 169,450 people in the US, with median pay of $50,690 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
