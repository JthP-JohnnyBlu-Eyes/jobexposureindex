Questions about construction managers and AI

Will AI replace construction managers?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. Construction Managers score 44 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #348 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 21 of the job's 25 tasks, 1 of them on its own, while 4 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which construction managers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Interpret and explain plans and contract terms to representatives of the owner or developer, including administrative staff, workers, or clients".

Which construction managers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Direct and supervise construction or related workers"; "Confer with supervisory personnel, owners, contractors, or design professionals to discuss and resolve matters, such as work procedures, complaints, or construction problems"; "Contract or oversee craft work, such as painting or plumbing".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 6 of the 25 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as construction managers, and what do they earn?

About 380,360 people in the US, with median pay of $114,990 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
