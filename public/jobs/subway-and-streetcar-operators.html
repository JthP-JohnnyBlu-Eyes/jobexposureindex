Questions about subway and streetcar operators and AI

Will AI replace subway and streetcar operators?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. Subway and Streetcar Operators score 41 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #406 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 5 of the job's 12 tasks, 5 of them on its own, while 7 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which subway and streetcar operators tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Report delays, mechanical problems, and emergencies to supervisors or dispatchers, using radios"; "Make announcements to passengers, such as notifications of upcoming stops or schedule delays"; "Complete reports, including shift summaries and incident or accident reports".

Which subway and streetcar operators tasks are hardest for AI?

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
