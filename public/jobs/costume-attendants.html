Questions about costume attendants and AI

Will AI replace costume attendants?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Costume Attendants score 26 out of 100 (moderate exposure), #570 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 8 of the job's 21 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 13 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which costume attendants tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Create worksheets for dressing lists, show notes, or costume checks"; "Distribute costumes or related equipment and keep records of item status".

Which costume attendants tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Provide dressing assistance to cast members or assign cast dressers to assist specific cast members with costume changes"; "Arrange costumes in order of use to facilitate quick-change procedures for performances"; "Design or construct costumes or send them to tailors for construction, major repairs, or alterations".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Not much yet. None of the job's tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude in April–May 2026, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as costume attendants, and what do they earn?

About 6,510 people in the US, with median pay of $50,400 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
