Questions about waiters and waitresses and AI

Will AI replace waiters and waitresses?

Unlikely. Most of the work is hard for today's AI to speed up. Waiters and Waitresses score 18 out of 100 (low exposure), #651 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 7 of the job's 25 tasks, 2 of them on its own, while 18 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which waiters and waitresses tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Write patrons' food orders on order slips, memorize orders, or enter orders into computers for transmittal to kitchen staff"; "Explain how various menu items are prepared, describing ingredients and cooking methods".

Which waiters and waitresses tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Take orders from patrons for food or beverages"; "Check with customers to ensure that they are enjoying their meals, and take action to correct any problems"; "Check patrons' identification to ensure that they meet minimum age requirements for consumption of alcoholic beverages".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 2 of the 25 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as waiters and waitresses, and what do they earn?

About 2,270,910 people in the US, with median pay of $35,230 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
