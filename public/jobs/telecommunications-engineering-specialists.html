Questions about telecommunications engineering specialists and AI

Will AI replace telecommunications engineering specialists?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Telecommunications Engineering Specialists score 61 out of 100 (high exposure), #92 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 23 of the job's 26 tasks, 9 of them on its own, while 3 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which telecommunications engineering specialists tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Document procedures for hardware and software installation and use"; "Install, or coordinate installation of, new or modified hardware, software, or programming modules of telecommunications systems"; "Implement or perform preventive maintenance, backup, or recovery procedures".

Which telecommunications engineering specialists tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Inspect sites to determine physical configuration, such as device locations and conduit pathways"; "Work with personnel and facilities management staff to install, remove, or relocate user connectivity equipment and devices"; "Supervise maintenance of telecommunications equipment".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 10 of the 26 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as telecommunications engineering specialists, and what do they earn?

About 179,740 people in the US (figure for Computer Network Architects), with median pay of $134,050 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
