Questions about new accounts clerks and AI

Will AI replace new accounts clerks?

Not entirely. Parts of the job are likely to change as AI takes over routine tasks. New Accounts Clerks score 56 out of 100 (elevated exposure), #129 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 13 of the job's 15 tasks, 4 of them on its own, while 2 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which new accounts clerks tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Compile information about new accounts, enter account information into computers, and file related forms or other documents"; "Inform customers of procedures for applying for services, such as ATM cards, direct deposit of checks, and certificates of deposit"; "Answer customers' questions and explain available services, such as deposit accounts, bonds, and securities".

Which new accounts clerks tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Perform teller duties as required"; "Issue initial and replacement safe-deposit keys to customers, and admit customers to vaults".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 5 of the 15 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as new accounts clerks, and what do they earn?

About 36,860 people in the US, with median pay of $47,670 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
