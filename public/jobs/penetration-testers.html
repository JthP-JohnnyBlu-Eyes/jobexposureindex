Questions about penetration testers and AI

Will AI replace penetration testers?

Probably not entirely, but the job is likely to change a lot. Penetration Testers score 80 out of 100 (very high exposure), #28 of 923 US jobs. AI could speed up 21 of the job's 22 tasks, 14 of them on its own, while 1 see little or no speed-up. Exposure means the work is likely to change, not that the job will disappear.

Which penetration testers tasks can AI do?

The most exposed tasks include "Conduct network and security system audits, using established criteria"; "Configure information systems to incorporate principles of least functionality and least access"; "Design security solutions to address known device vulnerabilities".

Which penetration testers tasks are hardest for AI?

Tasks with little or no AI speed-up include "Assess the physical security of servers, systems, or network devices to identify vulnerability to temperature, vandalism, or natural disasters".

Are people already using AI for this work?

Yes. In April–May 2026, 10 of the 22 tasks showed up in real conversations with Claude, according to the Anthropic Economic Index.

How many people work as penetration testers, and what do they earn?

About 435,370 people in the US (figure for Computer Occupations, All Other), with median pay of $116,580 a year (BLS, May 2025).

How to read this score. Exposure measures how much of the work AI could speed up, not whether the job will disappear. A highly exposed job can change shape, with people spending less time on routine tasks and more on the rest. The score counts tasks AI can speed up on its own in full, and tasks that need extra software at half weight.

The ratings come from published research based on GPT-4 (2023). To show what's happening now, each task is also checked against the latest Anthropic Economic Index, which records which tasks people actually use AI for.
